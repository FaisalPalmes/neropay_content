## Part 1 — The programme, exactly

### What it is

The NeroPay Partner Programme pays people and businesses for introducing UK merchants — restaurants, cafés, takeaways, shops, salons, trades — to NeroPay. NeroPay supplies the card terminal and free EPOS software, does the onboarding, setup and support. The partner only makes the introduction. There is no cost to join, no stock to buy, no sales target to keep the account, and the partner never handles the merchant's money.

Sign-up is at **partners.neropay.app**. A partner gets a personal referral link (a NeroPay merchant registration link carrying their partner reference) and a partner dashboard showing referrals, their status, and payouts. A merchant who registers through the link is attributed to the partner.

### What a partner earns — two separate things

**1. A bonus on every merchant. Guaranteed, per merchant, no minimum, no conditions beyond the merchant going live.**

Calculated from the merchant's total successful card processing in their **first 30 days** from registration:

| Merchant's first 30 days of processing | Partner bonus |
| --- | --- |
| £0 – £20,000 | £100 |
| £20,000.01 – £39,999.99 | £200 |
| £40,000 and above | £300 |

Introduce one merchant, get one bonus. Introduce ten, get ten. Nothing to unlock. This is the headline and the honest emotional core of every video: *every business you introduce pays you*.

**These tiers are final.** £300 is the top bonus. If you see "up to £500" in any older NeroPay material, it is superseded — never carry it into a video.

**2. Revenue share on the partner's whole network. Conditional, re-qualified every calendar month.**

| New active merchants introduced in the calendar month | Revenue share that month |
| --- | --- |
| 3 – 20 | 20% |
| 21 – 35 | 25% |
| 36 – 60 | 30% |
| 61 – 110 | 35% |
| 111 and above | 40% |

The rate is set by how many **new** active merchants were introduced that month, but it is paid on the activity of **every** merchant in the partner's network — not just the new three. A partner with forty merchants who introduces three more this month earns 20% across all forty. Three a month is a **gate**, not a cap. We call meeting it being an **Active Partner**.

An **active merchant** is one whose account is enabled and has taken at least one successful payment. Videos say "active" or "live and taking payments" and leave the precise definition to the T&Cs, which is where it belongs.

**What the percentage is a share of.** It is a share of **NeroPay's earnings from those merchants' card payments** — the revenue their processing generates for NeroPay. It is **not** a share of the merchants' turnover. Never say "a share of everything they process" or "20% of their sales"; say "a share of what your merchants' card payments earn NeroPay" or "a share of the revenue your network generates". This wording is the approved wording; use it as written.

**Revenue share is always expressed as a percentage with its condition, never as "£X a month".** It varies with each network, so no average pound figure is used in any video. Bonus arithmetic can be shown because it is per-unit and fixed.

Payouts show in the partner dashboard as **Partner Revenue Share** transactions. Bonuses are paid once the merchant's first 30 days are complete and the merchant is verified active. Videos do not describe payout timing or method — "paid to your partner account" is as specific as any script goes.

### Who partners actually are

Anyone who knows business owners. In practice the strongest are: EPOS installers and IT resellers; wholesalers and cash-and-carry reps who visit shops weekly; accountants and bookkeepers; community connectors (Turkish, Kurdish, Chinese, South Asian business communities in particular); ex-hospitality staff who know every restaurant on their high street; sales people with a side channel; students and recent graduates with time and a phone; delivery drivers who see fifty shop counters a day. The programme fits anyone who can make an introduction and a follow-up — it does not need selling skill, because NeroPay does the setup and the support.

### What NeroPay offers the merchant (so the partner has something to say)

UK card terminal, free EPOS software (NeroPOS), transparent pricing with no hidden fees, UK support, fast settlement. **Do not quote merchant prices, rates or savings in partner videos.** The partner video sells the introduction, not the terminal. "Transparent pricing" and "free EPOS software" are the only merchant-side claims permitted.

## Part 2 — The rules. Read twice.

Everything in this section exists because of things that have already gone wrong. In August a colleague posted a truthful partner-programme creative from her personal profile and Meta restricted her account for a month under "financial fraud and deception". The numbers were accurate. The **register** — big numbers, "passive income", "no effort, just an introduction", "no cost, no contract" — matched the pattern of a scam, and classifiers do not check truth. The UK ASA applies the same tests to business-opportunity advertising. These rules are how we make appealing videos that survive both.

### The five hard rules

1. **Every number travels with its condition, in the same sentence, spoken and on screen.** "Twenty per cent once you're introducing three a month." Never "earn 20%" or "up to 40%" on its own. A super nobody can read at normal speed counts as absent — hold it.
2. **Per-unit, never total.** "You earn £100 to £300 for every merchant you introduce" is fine, because it is a price per introduction. "You'll earn £900 a month" is not. Show arithmetic as arithmetic — three merchants × £100–£300 — and let the viewer do the sum; do not present the sum as their income. Never say "guaranteed income". The bonus is guaranteed *per merchant*; say that.
3. **The words "passive income", "easy money", "no effort", "risk-free", "no cost no contract" do not appear, in any language, on screen or in voice.** The feeling of recurring income is built by *structure* (see Part 3: the ledger that keeps ticking, "while they keep trading, you keep earning — as long as you're bringing three a month") not by those phrases. "Recurring", "ongoing", "keeps coming", "every month you qualify" are the words.
4. **No lifestyle imagery next to a figure.** No cars, watches, holidays, beaches, stacks of cash, phones showing bank balances. The visual language is the ledger, the counter, the high street, the till, the terminal, the map of shops — money as the consequence of real businesses trading.
5. **No targeting by personal attribute, in copy or on screen.** Never "unemployed?", "on benefits?", "broke?", "student?", "immigrant?", "over 50?". Meta rejects ads that assert or imply personal characteristics, and it reads the video. Audiences are reached by **situation and setting** ("you know three cafés", "you're in shops all week", "you do the books for twenty businesses") and by Meta targeting on the campaign side, never by naming the person. Hooks in Part 3 are written this way; keep it that way when you write more.

### The compliance line

Every social video ends on, and every description carries, this line — verbatim in the description, short form as the final super:

> **Full:** Bonus paid per verified active merchant, £100–£300 based on their total processing volume in their first 30 days. Revenue share requires a minimum of three new active merchants in the same calendar month; the rate depends on how many are introduced that month. Full terms at partners.neropay.app.
>
> **Short (final super, held 3s minimum):** £100–£300 per merchant. Revenue share needs 3+ new active merchants per calendar month. Terms: partners.neropay.app

The long-form video carries the full line as its final frame and in the description.

### Voice and identity

- **The company speaks as "we".** Never Faisal, never a named employee, never "I".
- **Plain British English.** Sentence case. Contractions are fine. No exclamation marks in supers. No "simply", "genuinely", "unlock", "game-changer", "hustle".
- **Human means specific.** "The café by the station that still only takes cash" beats "local businesses". "The bloke who did your shop's EPOS" beats "business connections". Write to one person with one shop in mind.
- **Warm, not hype.** The tone of a friend telling you about something that worked for them, not a recruiter. Confidence comes from the honesty — we volunteer the three-a-month condition rather than hide it, and that is a selling point: "your bonus is yours either way".
- **If the voiceover is AI-generated**, say so in the description ("Voice generated with AI"). Consistent with our AI-presenter disclosure rule. No AI presenter on screen in these — they are pure motion graphics.
- **Claims register.** Keep a `claims.md` beside the renders listing every figure that appears on screen, the source (this brief, Part 1) and the date. It is the substantiation record if anyone asks.

### Turkish later

Scripts are written so every figure and condition is in a single sentence that can be translated as a unit. Keep supers as separate strings in the build script so the Turkish pass changes strings, not layouts. Turkish never runs from a personal profile and never in community groups as broadcast — Page only, and only after the English set has run clean for two weeks.

## Part 3 — Design system and pipeline

### Brand

| Token | Value | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ground | `#0A0B0D` | Every frame's base. Social and long-form both dark-first |
| Ink | `#FFFFFF` | Primary text |
| Dim | `#8A8F97` | Secondary text, conditions, rules, axis labels |
| Accent | `#F5C518` | One accent element per frame. The number, the cursor, the rule, or the CTA — never two |
| Card | `#141619` | Surfaces (ledger rows, tiles) |
| Good | `#2FBF71` | Only for "succeeded" / tick states in the ledger. Never as decoration |

Type: **Inter Display** for headlines and numbers (tight tracking, weight 600–700), **Inter** for body and supers (weight 400–500), **DejaVu Sans Mono** for ledger rows, references and the compliance super. Tabular numerals everywhere digits move. Yellow block cursor for typewriter builds (existing pattern from the end sequence).

Motion: builds are mask-up, typewriter (2 frames per character at 25fps), count-up with ease-out over 18–24 frames, rule scale from the left, cross-dissolve 8 frames between scenes. Nothing bounces. Nothing spins. The signature move is the **ledger tick**: a row slides in from below, its status pill flips grey → green, and the running total in the corner counts up. It is the whole programme in one gesture and it is reused in both briefs.

### Two visual metaphors, used consistently

- **The ledger.** A dark table of rows: merchant type (never a real name — "Café · Stockport", "Barber · Leeds"), date, event ("Went live", "Bonus £100", "Partner Revenue Share"), status pill. This is what the partner dashboard feels like, rendered as motion. It carries the bonus story and the recurring story without a single lifestyle image.
- **The map / the street.** A row of simplified shopfront tiles (a café, a takeaway, a barber, a corner shop, a salon), each lighting up yellow when "introduced". Counts of three become visible as three tiles lighting up. This carries the "you already know these people" story.

Invent every merchant. Use trading-type + town, never a name. Never show a real NeroPay merchant, a real balance, or a real partner.

### No static tables. Two animated modules carry the tiers instead.

The tier tables in Part 1 are the **source**, not the graphic. On screen, earnings are never a table. They are a journey from the base to the top, animated so the viewer sees the condition move *with* the number the whole way. This is both more engaging and more honest than a table, because at no point is a figure on screen without the volume or count that earns it.

**Module 1 — the Bonus Dial** (used wherever the bonus is explained)

A single merchant's first 30 days, as a horizontal volume track with a travelling marker. The bonus figure sits above the marker, large, in accent. The volume figure sits below it, in dim tabular numerals.

| Beat | Frames (25fps) | Volume readout (below) | Bonus (above) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | 0–20 | £0 | £100 | Marker at the left. Hold so the pair is read together: "from the first pound they take, you're on £100". |
| Travel 1 | 20–60 | counts £0 → £20,000 | £100 held | The marker travels; the bonus does **not** change until the threshold. Ease-out. |
| Step 1 | 60–80 | £20,000 | flips to £200 | A ledger tick sound. Threshold label appears on the track: "£20k". |
| Travel 2 | 80–120 | counts £20,000 → £40,000 | £200 held | |
| Step 2 | 120–140 | £40,000 | flips to £300 | Tick. Threshold label "£40k". |
| Rest | 140–200 | "£40,000+" | £300 | The end frame holds **≥ 2.4s** with three things visible: **£300 · £40,000+ in their first 30 days · per merchant**. Never rest on "£300" alone. |

The rest frame is the only place "up to" is permitted, as a dim super: *"up to £300 per merchant — £40,000+ in their first 30 days"*. Both halves in one line. A viewer who freeze-frames anywhere on the dial sees a volume and the bonus that volume earns — that is what keeps it true.

**Module 2 — the Rate Climb** (used wherever revenue share is explained)

The same idea for the monthly count. A vertical or diagonal path of shopfront tiles lighting up as a counter of *new active merchants this month* climbs; the rate steps beside it. Start at **0** with the rate blank and a dim "not yet — 3 to qualify". At **3** the "Active Partner" badge lights and the rate shows **20%**. The counter then climbs, with the rate stepping at 21 (25%), 36 (30%), 61 (35%) and 111 (40%), each step held 15 frames with the count visible beside the rate. Rest frame (≥ 2.4s): **40% · 111+ new active merchants in one month**, and beneath it, dim: *"Most partners start at 20%: three a month."* That last line is mandatory — it stops the top rate reading as typical.

Rules for both modules: the climb always starts at the base, never at the top; the counter never skips a threshold; the number and its condition are never more than one line apart; the rest frame is the last thing seen before the cut. In short-form, Module 2 may be shortened to base → 20% → rest at 40% with "111+" showing, provided the 21/36/61 steps are visibly passed on the path (dim labels), not omitted. In the long-form, run it in full.

### Pipeline

Same pattern as the NeroPay One end sequence: each scene is an HTML file with a `setFrame(n)` function; `anim.py` screenshots frames in Playwright and ffmpeg encodes. Build every text string as a variable at the top of the build script (`strings_en.py`, later `strings_tr.py`). Master at 1920×1080 25fps; export 1080×1920, 1080×1350 and 1080×1080 from one master via `edit.py`; the 9:16 crop is the primary social deliverable, so compose safe for it (title-safe area: centre 1080×1350 of the 16:9 frame, text never in the outer 15% horizontally). Captions burned in as the ASS track. Sound: one low tone under the hook, the ledger tick (a soft mechanical click) on every row, a single soft thud on the CTA cut. No music bed unless Faisal supplies a licensed track. Loudness normalised for social.

Deliver each render with the `qc-contact.png` contact sheet and a `claims.md`.

### The goal of every social video: win the first three seconds, then the next five

This is the standing instruction for **all** social-media-aimed videos, present and future. Every one opens with a **pattern interrupt** — something that makes a thumb stop — and then plants a reason to stay for the body. Three kinds of opener are approved. Mix them across the set; never open two consecutive posts with the same kind.

| Kind | What it is | Why it holds attention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The question** | A direct question the viewer answers in their head before they can stop themselves. "Are you the person everyone asks about card machines?" | A question creates an open loop. The body closes it. |
| **The "you could be earning…"** | A per-unit earnings line tied to a **specific relationship or place** the viewer has. "You could be earning £100 to £300 for the café you're stood in." | It attaches money to something they already have, which is the entire programme in one sentence. Always per merchant, always with the condition super. |
| **The absurd-but-true statement** | A line that sounds like an exaggeration and is literally accurate. "The chip shop owes you money. It just doesn't know it yet." | Surprise is the strongest scroll-stopper, and because it is true it survives the fact-check the comments will run. |

The rules of Part 2 apply *inside* the hook, not just after it. So a question never names a personal attribute ("Are you unemployed?" is out; "Are you the one your mates ask about card fees?" is in). A "you could be earning" line is always per merchant, never a monthly total, and its super carries "£100–£300 per merchant · first 30 days" on the same frame. An absurd line is only allowed if it is literally true.

**Keep them for five more seconds.** A hook alone buys three seconds. At the cut to B1, the first line of the body must *answer or escalate* the hook rather than start over — so B1's opening line is swapped per hook (a one-string variable in `strings_en.py`), e.g. after a question: "Then here's how the NeroPay Partner Programme pays you for that."; after "you could be earning": "That's the NeroPay Partner Programme. Here's the whole thing."; after an absurd line: "Here's why that's true." The visual should also carry a second beat at 3–4s — a count-up starting, a second tile lighting, a ledger row sliding in — so there is *movement* the moment the hook lands.

**Intensity, not volume.** High-intensity means a big single number, a hard cut, a typewriter hitting a full stop, a tick sound — not shouting, exclamation marks, or emoji. The register stays dry; the pace does the work.

## Part 6 — QA before anything leaves the machine

Run this on every export. A single fail is a fail.

1. Every figure on screen has its condition on screen at the same time, legible at 1× speed on a phone. Freeze-frame each figure and read the frame. For the two modules specifically: scrub the Bonus Dial and the Rate Climb frame by frame — at every frame the bonus/rate must sit with the volume/count that earns it, the climb must start at the base, no threshold may be skipped, and the rest frame must carry the "£40,000+ / 111+" condition and (for the Rate Climb) the "Most partners start at 20%" line.
