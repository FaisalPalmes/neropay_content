The gate, in one paragraph

A 5,000 sq ft gym lives or dies on its building. The wrong unit adds a five-month planning application with a real chance of refusal, a £25,000 power connection, a changing block that cannot drain, a ceiling too low for a rack, and a car park that empties the catchment. None of those is fixable with marketing. So the order is: find a unit that passes every must-have below, agree X, and only then read the costs, sequence and model pages with that X plugged in.

This is also why the operating budget of £10,000–£15,000 a month that started the research was the wrong frame. That budget is what the gym costs to run once it exists. It does not open anything. Opening is a capital question, and the premises decide how large.

Foundry opens in Stockport town centre in November 2026

24/7, £31.99 rolling, no contract, no joining fee, Hammer Strength kit, boxing ring, showers, free parking, 27 UK sites behind it. It takes the £30–£35 24/7 slot in the town centre before any date NeroGym could open. The premises answer is to be somewhere Foundry, PureGym and The Gym Group are not, which points north and towards the A34 corridor, and to sell something they do not: space per member and a room that is looked after.

Planning: the one thing to check on every building, every time

Gyms are Use Class E(d). Movement inside Class E is not development and needs no permission. So an office, a shop, a trade counter or a light industrial unit (E(g)(iii)) can become a gym as of right.

General industrial (B2) and storage or distribution (B8) were deliberately left outside Class E in the 2020 reforms, and no permitted development right takes B2 or B8 into Class E. A unit in either needs a full change-of-use application: £610 fee from April 2026, three to five months, and gyms get refused on parking and highways. Councils have approved industrial-estate gyms only with transport plans that cap membership at a level no business survives.

Which class a unit is in turns on its lawful planning use, not on what the agent's particulars say. Most Stockport estates hold a mix. A Certificate of Lawfulness of Proposed Use (CLOPUD) is the cheap way to be certain: a few hundred pounds and about eight weeks, and it is the first thing to ask an agent for.

Correction to the September brief. The "Priors Way industrial estate" approval it cites, with an ~80-member cap, is in Maidenhead (Royal Borough of Windsor and Maidenhead), not Stockport, and the cap could not be verified from the local press report. The point stands as an example of how planning conditions can strangle a gym; it is not a Stockport precedent.

Location criteria

Must-haves are pass or fail. Should-haves are scored. A unit that fails one must-have is not viewed twice, whatever the rent.

  • 01
    Lawful use in Class E, or a change of use already granted

    Confirmed by the planning history or a CLOPUD, not by the brochure. B2/B8 only if the landlord will carry the application and the rent-free period covers it.

    must
  • 02
    4,500–6,000 sq ft on one level, ground floor

    Smaller and the capacity promise cannot pay the rent; larger and the fit-out outruns the capital. A mezzanine is store room, not gym floor: the loading rarely passes.

    must
  • 03
    Clear height of 4 m or more under any services

    Racks, pull-up bars, overhead press and the indirect lighting scheme all need it. Industrial eaves at 6–7 m are ideal. No basements.

    must
  • 04
    Free parking for 25+ cars on site or immediately adjacent

    80.4% of Stockport households own a vehicle. Free parking is the structural advantage over every town-centre operator, and the thing planners test hardest.

    must
  • 05
    Three-phase supply, or a confirmed quote from Electricity North West under £8,000

    Cardio, ventilation, hot water and a heat pump together need it. Ask the landlord for the meter details before the first viewing.

    must
  • 06
    Foul drainage that can serve a changing block where the plan wants it

    Relocating a drainage run through a slab is one of the two costs that most often doubles a fit-out. An existing toilet core in the right corner is worth £20,000.

    must
  • 07
    Ground-level loading access

    A leg press is 400 kg. A roller-shutter or double door at slab level, with lorry access, or the install bill doubles.

    must
  • 08
    Lease permits 24-hour trading and Sunday trading, with no estate gate locked at night

    Some estates close a barrier at 19:00. Check the estate regulations, not just the lease.

    must
  • 09
    Within a ten-minute drive of 40,000+ residents and at the seam of an employment cluster

    Daytime trade from the offices next door, evening trade from the streets behind. An estate that empties at 18:00 with no housing within a mile fails the evening; a purely residential parade fails the afternoon.

    should
  • 10
    Visible from a main road with a fascia the sign can go on

    The lit N is the cheapest advertising the group will ever buy. A unit at the back of an estate needs an estate-entrance sign in the lease.

    should
  • 11
    Not within a mile of PureGym, The Gym Group or Foundry

    The SK8 Cheadle corridor has no PureGym. North Stockport has PureGym Stockport North on Didsbury Road; keep clear of it.

    should
  • 12
    Roof that will take 30–50 kWp of solar

    A 24/7 gym has an unusual daytime baseload. £35,000–£55,000 of panels pays back in five to eight years and takes the energy line from £3,000 to nearer £2,000 a month.

    should
  • 13
    Gigabit fibre available at the address

    Door, cameras, screens and the app depend on it. Check Openreach and CityFibre availability before offering.

    should
  • 14
    Landlord who will fund or part-fund the changing block against a longer term

    Common on industrial estates being repositioned. Worth a year of rent-free in effect.

    should

Where in Stockport

Site north, not south, and not in the town centre. The Stockport constituency's 25–34 share is 15.6% against 13.3% regionally; Portwood is among the 25 most deprived neighbourhoods in England and is the wrong catchment for a £45–£50 product; the town centre now has three budget 24/7 gyms within half a mile of each other.

Eray's separate point about siting near employers holds, with one qualification: an office catchment empties at 18:00, so the unit has to sit where jobs and homes meet.

Cheadle Royal / A34 / Bird Hall Lane
The standout gap. Large employment cluster, Cheadle and Cheadle Hulme housing behind it, no PureGym in SK8. Class E stock exists here as offices and trade counters as well as light industrial. Needs a Class E agent briefed specifically.
Bredbury (Balmoral, Bredbury Park Way, Horsfield Way)
The only published in-band rent (Balmoral, 5,824 sq ft, £3,750 pcm). Strong parking, M60 J25 access, mixed B2/B8 and light industrial, so the use-class question is live on every unit. Housing in Bredbury and Woodley behind it.
Reddish / Houldsworth
Mill conversions with the ceiling height and the look the concept wants; Life Leisure is present, PureGym is not. Check floor loading and lifts.
Heaton Chapel / Heaton Moor edge
Right demographic, thin industrial stock, close to PureGym Stockport North. Only if a unit appears on the Wellington Road side.
Hazel Grove (A6 corridor)
Good residential catchment, X Fitness already serves lifters there, and it is the south. Lower priority.

What X does to the rest of the brief

Every £1 of monthly rent needs about 3p of net membership revenue to cover it, which sounds small until it is multiplied out. The table uses the mid running-cost case and the Strength House price plan; change either on the model page.

Rent is not the whole premises cost. Service charge and insurance rent (£1.00–£1.50 per sq ft a year), business rates on a rent-derived rateable value (£955–£1,433 a month at the leisure multiplier), and the deposit (three to six months of X, held) all move with it.

Lease terms to hold out for

Term and break
Ten years with a tenant-only break at year five. Landlords of secondary industrial stock will trade a longer term for a lower rent or a bigger rent-free period; take the rent-free.
Rent-free
Six months, framed as the fit-out period. Three is the floor. Every month of rent-free is a month of X off the capital requirement.
Deposit
Three months, not six, held in a stakeholder account. A new subsidiary with no accounts will be asked for six or a parent guarantee from Nero Panda Ltd, which is exactly the diligence question on the overview page.
Permitted use
"Use within Class E including as a gym and fitness centre open 24 hours", written in. Not "light industrial".
Alterations
Consent for the changing block, three-phase upgrade, external signage and roof-mounted solar agreed in the heads of terms, with a reinstatement cap so the dilapidations at exit are not the cost of removing a changing room.
Repairing
Internal repairing only if it can be got; full repairing and insuring is normal on a whole unit, so the roof condition survey matters before signing.
Rent review
Year five, open market, upwards only is the norm. Ask for a cap linked to CPI.
Assignment
Right to assign or underlet with consent not unreasonably withheld. A gym fit-out has resale value to another operator only if the lease travels with it.

Scoring a unit
