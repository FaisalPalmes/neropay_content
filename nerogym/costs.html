How to read it

Rows are in the order the money leaves, grouped by the phases on the sequence page. Each row names the item, what drives the price, who supplies it in the UK (and overseas where that makes sense), and the source the figure was read from. "est." marks a figure nobody publishes, built from the sources beside it. Everything was read from search-result snippets on the date above because direct page fetches were blocked in the research session; a lender will want the live page.

The tiers are not good, better, best. Value is a real gym that would open and trade, and it is roughly the September model's Format B. Mid is the concept on the concept page. Premium is what the brief's mood boards cost if taken literally.

Buying from China directly

The value tier's strength stations assume a container from a reputable OEM. It is a real option and it roughly halves the strength bill. It also makes NeroGym the importer of record with product liability, no UK service network and no recourse on a 30/70 payment split. Here is the arithmetic and the trade-offs, so it is chosen rather than stumbled into.

Who
DHZ Fitness, Impulse, Shua, Land Fitness, BFT, Realleader are the reputable tier. The US$295–345 plate-loaded listings on Made-in-China (Kasung, 2026) are the bottom tier and the upholstery, cables and pulleys show it.
Price
Reputable-tier FOB (estimate): US$700–1,500 plate-loaded, US$900–2,000 selectorised, US$2,500–4,500 commercial treadmill.
Freight
40 ft high-cube Shanghai or Ningbo to Felixstowe US$2,600–5,550 base ocean, 30–40 days, moving 20–30% within a month; 25–35 machines per container part-disassembled; UK port, haulage to Stockport and customs entry £900–£1,650 (estimate).
Duty and VAT
HS 9506.91: 2.7% of customs value per a UK sourcing guide, possibly 2.0% on the UK Global Tariff (the tariff site could not be reached; check trade-tariff.service.gov.uk). Import VAT 20% on CIF plus duty, recoverable under postponed VAT accounting.
Landed example
A US$900 FOB machine lands at roughly £860 ex VAT (£680 machine, £120 freight share, £22 duty, £40 port) against £2,800–£3,500 for the UK mid tier.
Compliance
UKCA mark and a UK Declaration of Conformity; EN ISO 20957 (ex EN 957) test reports, which are self-declared and need reading; electrical items also BS EN 60335-1; GPSR 2005. CE reports are reusable for UKCA self-declaration.
Lead time
Production 30–60 days, sea 30–40, clearance 5–10: ten to sixteen weeks. Order in Phase 2.
The honest trade
55–70% below UK mid-tier landed. Against: pre-shipment inspection (US$300–500 a day) is not optional; spares are a container away; a snapped cable on a station with no UK parts is a week of downtime against a 48-hour promise. Sensible for benches, racks, plate-loaded frames. Not sensible for cardio or anything with a stack and a pulley.

Financing the kit

The ledger assumes the equipment is bought outright. Asset finance would take most of the equipment line out of the capital figure and add a monthly cost that is not on the running-costs ledger. Terms seen in September 2026: 36–60 months, deposit from 0%, decisions in hours; finance lease payments 10–20% below hire purchase, which ends with a £1 option to buy. Retailers offer 0% over 6–24 months on smaller orders and up to 60 months at 12.9% APR. Bank Rate is 3.75% (held 30 July 2026; next decision 17 September). A new-start gym should expect 9–15% APR on HP, an established one 7–10%.

Illustration: of mid-tier kit over five years at 9% is roughly £4,000 a month. The model page has a toggle for it. Full expensing lets a company deduct 100% of the cost in year one either way.

Why this is higher than the September model

The September model put Format B, the strength gym at £49.99, at £394,100 to open. The value tier here without the contingency and reserve phase is about £366,000, which is the same figure with the lines shown. The mid tier is about and the difference is five decisions, each of which can be argued back:

  • Ventilation and heating at £60,000 rather than splits and a fan. A 24/7 room that smells of a 24/7 room is a churn cost, not a saving. Still the first line to take three quotes on.
  • A changing block built to Approved Document T with two single-sex ranges, at £60,000. The regulation applies on change of use and retrofitting it costs more than building it.
  • New mid-tier equipment at £195,000 rather than refurbished and imported at £105,000. This is the concept's "kit that works" promise; the refurbished route is a legitimate way to open cheaper and upgrade from cash flow.
  • The lighting scheme at £30,000 rather than £12,000 of high-bays. It is the only line on the ledger that a member photographs.
  • A cash reserve and a contingency, which the September figure did not carry as lines. They are not extra cost; they are the same risk written down.

The model page lets Eray pick a tier and see the break-even it produces. The point of the three columns is that the choice is his, made with the consequences visible, rather than a single number that turns out to have assumed the cheap answer to every question.
