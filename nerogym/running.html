The monthly ledger

Ex VAT. Rent first, then everything else in the order a bookkeeper would meet it. Marketing is shown for scale but the model treats it as a variable: churn × members × cost per join.

Not on this ledger: equipment finance (the one-off page assumes outright purchase; five years at 9% on mid-tier kit is about £4,000 a month), owner drawings, corporation tax (19% to £50k profit, 25% above £250k, 26.5% marginal between), and VAT, which is one sixth of every membership payment and is paid over quarterly.

Staffing, the largest line

National Living Wage is £12.71 an hour from 1 April 2026; the 18–20 rate is £10.85. Employer National Insurance is 15% above £5,000 a year per employee, with a £10,500 Employment Allowance that roughly cancels it at this headcount. Pension is 3% of qualifying earnings. Irregular-hours staff accrue holiday at 12.07%. Greater Manchester market rates (estimates; job boards could not be read in the session): general manager £30–38k, duty manager £25–29k, hosts and Level 2 instructors £12.71–£13.50 an hour, cleaner the same.

The lean model is what makes a £34.99 price arithmetically possible and it is also what the Henry Best inquest was about. A cleaner counted as "staffed", an alarm nobody used, an AED nobody could see, paramedics three minutes late because nobody met them. Lean is only defensible with the remote monitoring line funded (it is, at £750 a month in that column), lanyards on every member overnight, and an 18+ rule out of hours. Mid is the recommendation.

Personal trainers are not on the payroll. Six licensed PTs pay a fixed monthly rent under an agreement drafted by an employment solicitor, on the income side below.

Energy, the second

Small-business electricity was 27–30p per kWh in September 2026 (August average 29.6p) plus the Climate Change Levy at 0.801p and a standing charge of 45–70p a day. Gas was 8.5–9p. A 24/7 gym of this size is modelled at 75,000–110,000 kWh of electricity a year: the CIBSE TM46 fitness benchmark (160 kWh/m² electricity, 200 thermal) gives 74,400 kWh; published 24-hour gym averages run about 125,000; strength-led with LED and few treadmills sits between. Hot water for 40 showers a day is roughly 43,000 kWh thermal a year on its own.

Three things move this line more than any supplier switch: LED throughout (in the build), a heat pump for the hot water rather than an electric bank, and 30–50 kWp of solar on the roof at £850–£1,100 per kWp installed, which pays back in five to eight years and faster still on a building that uses power at midday.

Business rates

Rateable value is set from the rent at 1 April 2024. Gyms qualify for the retail, hospitality and leisure multipliers in 2026/27: 38.2p in the pound below £51,000 rateable value, 43.0p above. If Stockport does not accept the gym as leisure, the standard small-business multiplier is 43.2p. Small Business Rate Relief ends at £15,000 rateable value and does not help here. At a rateable value of £35,000 (5,000 sq ft at £7) the bill is £13,370 a year, £1,114 a month. Transitional relief may cap the year-one movement after the 2026 revaluation.

The cost of the 48-hour promise

The concept's first commitment is that a fault is fixed or the station replaced within 48 hours. That is bought with the maintenance reserve line (£1,000 a month mid: two planned visits a year at £60–£120 a machine, parts at 1.5–2% of fleet value, cables every two to three years, upholstery at 10% of pads a year), a shelf of common spares (cables, pulleys, pins, pads, about £2,000 in the opening stock), and a duplicate of every station people queue for in the opening kit list. Reactive-only servicing costs 25–30% more per machine per year than planned, according to the PSLT sector report in 2026, so the reserve is cheaper than not having it.

Income that is not memberships

Monthly, at steady state. The model adds these to membership contribution before it finds break-even. They are small against a £20,000 cost base and the largest is the one that carries legal risk.
