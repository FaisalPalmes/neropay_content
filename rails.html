The rails

These aren't preferences. Two of them carry criminal or account-ending consequences, and one of them has already cost NeroPay a restricted profile.

01

Non-negotiable

Criminal exposure

NeroFinance never appears

Merchant cash advance, terminal finance, any credit or lending language. Not in a post, not in a caption, not on a page a post links to. Promoting it unauthorised is capable of being an offence under s.21 FSMA.

Account-ending

No earnings claims, anywhere

No "passive income", "no effort", "risk-free", and no large money figures as the biggest element on an image. This is exactly what got a NeroPay profile restricted on 21 August under Meta's fraud and deception policy. The terms being true is not a defence — the classifier matches language patterns, not facts.

Nothing commercial from a personal profile

The Page posts. Always. A restriction on a personal profile reaches everything that profile administers, which is precisely how the August incident happened.

AI presenter disclosure

Any video with a generated presenter carries a clear on-screen note in the first three seconds. ASA guidance of 22 June 2026 and EU AI Act Article 50, in force since 2 August 2026 — the latter matters from the German launch onward.

Every competitor figure dated and sourced

"Correct as of [date]", from the provider's own published page. The screenshots are the substantiation record under the CAP Code and have to exist before publishing, not after a complaint.

No merchant named without written consent

Filming, name, business name and every channel it appears on. Signed before the shoot, not after.

No real data, ever

Demo accounts with fake figures only. Never the live dashboard, never KYC documents, never real transaction data — including in a background, including blurred.

No guaranteed savings

Not stated, not implied. Every educational post concedes a case where the reader should stay where they are, which is both honest and what makes the rest believable.

Write organic to ad standards

Third person, product-focused, no personal-attributes phrasing — nothing like "struggling with fees?". Organic posts aren't held to ad policy, but any of these might be boosted later, and writing them compliantly makes that a decision rather than a rewrite.

Never publish negotiated terms

No bespoke rates, margins, partner commission or commercial terms, and no merchant's name, without explicit permission.

02

What to measure, what to ignore

Social at NeroPay can't be judged on leads, because that isn't its job — and holding it to that standard is how it gets cancelled in November for failing at something it was never doing.

  • Saves and shares, not likes. A save on the statement carousel means someone intends to check their own bill. That's the behaviour the whole plan exists to create.
  • Profile visits after field days. Compare the days out on the road with the days not. If the credibility job is working, this is where it shows.
  • Partner conversations originated on LinkedIn. Count them by hand. There'll be few enough to name individually, and one wholesaler relationship is worth more than every other metric here combined.
  • "I saw your video" in a sales call. Not in any dashboard. Log it anyway — it's the only direct evidence the content is doing its job.
  • Follower count: ignore. A thousand followers who aren't merchants in Greater Manchester is worse than a hundred who are.

One review point: end of November, twelve weeks in. Not sooner — there's nothing to learn from four weeks and every temptation to change course on noise. If by then no partner conversation has come from LinkedIn and no merchant has mentioned the videos, cut back to two posts a month so the profile isn't dead, and put the hours into field and partners instead.

Sources: ASA guidance on AI-generated advertising, 22 June 2026. EU AI Act Article 50, in force 2 August 2026. Meta Community Standards, Fraud and Scams. s.21 Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. CAP Code substantiation requirements.
